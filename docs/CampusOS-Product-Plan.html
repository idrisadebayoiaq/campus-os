1. Summary and decisions

This document turns your ideas into a plan we can build in order. It covers five things: what to borrow from Sckooly, a separate CampusOS Business app, one website, a new theme, and a carefully designed campus community.

Decisions you have already made

TopicDecision
ThemePreview all three colour directions side by side before choosing (section 5).
Business appA separate app, "CampusOS Business", downloaded from the CampusOS landing page. The web portal stays for desktop jobs.
Sckooly-inspired featuresNote-based tutor, paid classes, timed quizzes, class rep and union roles, and a school community. I have filtered and ordered them in section 4.
CommunityNeeds a full plan before any code. Section 9 is that plan.

My recommended order

  1. Foundations (2 days): reconnect tools, rotate old secrets, redeploy portal and website.
  2. New theme (4 to 5 days): done first so every new screen and the Business app inherit it.
  3. Learning upgrades (12 to 16 days): note-based tutor, timed quizzes, class rep roles.
  4. Paid classes (6 to 8 days): new income for lecturers and for CampusOS.
  5. CampusOS Business app (22 to 30 days in three steps): restaurants first.
  6. One website (3 to 4 days): runs alongside the Business app so the download page is ready on launch day.
  7. Campus community (pilot in 15 to 20 days, then grow): built last because it needs moderation tools, a pilot campus and school partners to be safe.
Why this order. The theme is cheap and affects everything. The tutor, quizzes and paid classes build on tables and screens we already have, so they ship fast and earn money. The Business app protects the revenue we already depend on (food orders and housing). The community is the most valuable long-term feature but also the riskiest, so it gets the most preparation and a small pilot first.

What I need from you

  • Pick a theme after seeing section 5.
  • Confirm the commission and payout rules for paid classes (section 8.4).
  • Choose the pilot campus and a school contact for the community (section 9.12).
  • Answer the open decisions in section 11.

6. CampusOS Business app

6.1 Purpose and principles

  • Operations first. The app does the daily jobs a business owner does on a phone. Big setup and reporting stay on the web portal.
  • Never miss an order. Loud, reliable push notifications are the main reason this app exists.
  • Same backend. Same Supabase project, same accounts, same wallet and payout rules. No duplicate logic.
  • Business accounts only. One account type per user is already enforced, so the app signs in business, lecturer and team accounts, and politely redirects students to the student app.
  • Safe money actions. Payout requests ask for the payment password or fingerprint, reusing the flow we just built.

8. Feature specifications

8.1 Note-based tutor ("Study from my notes")

What the student sees

  1. In the AI tutor or Library, tap Add a note: choose a PDF, take photos of handwritten pages, or paste text.
  2. The app shows the note with four actions: Revise with me, Summarise, Make flashcards, Practice questions.
  3. Revise with me works like a guided class: the tutor splits the note into sections, explains one point at a time, checks understanding with a short question, then moves on. Progress is saved.
  4. Summarise gives a one-page summary with headings, key terms table and a "memorise this" quote box.
  5. Flashcards are saved straight into the existing flashcards screen.
  6. Practice questions are saved into the CBT practice screen with answers and explanations.

How it works

  • Files go to a private storage folder. A server function extracts text (PDF text first; photos read by a vision-capable model). Text is split into sections and stored.
  • Version 1 sends the relevant sections to the model directly (enough for typical lecture notes). Version 2 adds embeddings for very long notes.
  • Strict rule in the instructions: answer only from the note, say clearly when something is outside the note, never invent references.
  • Uses the formatting we just built, so tables, diagrams and quote boxes appear in the answers.

Data and limits

  • New tables: notes, note sections, note activity (progress). Notes are private to the student and can be deleted at any time.
  • Limits to control cost: free students get a small number of notes and pages per month; Premium gets more. Exam-prep students follow the same rules as their tutor access.
  • Never study another student's note: strict row-level security.

Effort: 5 to 7 days.

8.2 Timed quizzes in classes

  • Lecturer: creates a quiz with multiple-choice questions, a time limit, an open and close window, shuffling, and number of attempts. Can paste a note and let AI draft questions, then reviews and edits them before publishing.
  • Student: sees open quizzes in the class, starts the quiz, a server-side timer runs, answers save as they go, results show immediately or after the window (lecturer's choice).
  • Marking: automatic. Scores flow into the class grades table already in use.
  • Fairness: the timer and the answer key stay on the server, questions and options are shuffled, one active attempt, and the screen is protected from screenshots during a quiz on Android.
  • Insights: average score, hardest questions, who has not taken it, and a "needs help" list for the lecturer.
  • New tables: quizzes, quiz questions, quiz attempts and answers.

Effort: 4 to 5 days.

8.3 Class rep and student union roles

Class rep

  • A lecturer or the school authority nominates a student in a class. The student accepts.
  • Powers (class only): post announcements, run attendance, share materials, start a live session, and post as "Class rep" in the class space of the community.
  • Cannot see grades or private student data. The nominator can remove the role at any time.

Student union (organisation)

  • An organisation attached to one school, applied for through the portal and verified by the school authority (same approval pattern as other applications).
  • Gets free rooms, a verified "Student union" badge, an official channel in the community, and a small team of officers with roles.
  • Misuse rules: an officer who breaks guidelines loses posting rights, and the school authority can suspend the whole account.

Effort: 3 to 4 days (plus the community connection in section 9).

8.4 Paid classes

Flow

  1. A verified lecturer or trainer sets a class to Paid with a monthly price. Schools and unions run free classes.
  2. The student finds the class in search (by school, course, lecturer, price) or with a code, and sees what is included.
  3. To join, the student pays the first month from the wallet (payment password or fingerprint). Access starts immediately.
  4. Each month the system renews automatically from the wallet. If the wallet is short, the student gets reminders and a short grace period, then access pauses (nothing is lost).
  5. The student can cancel any time. Access continues until the end of the paid month.

Money rules (proposed)

  • Student: class search, class page with price and preview, "My subscriptions" with renewal date and cancel.
  • Lecturer (app and portal): price setting, members list, monthly income, new and cancelled members, failed renewals.

9. Campus community: the full plan

The idea in one line. A verified, school-based feed where students get real campus news, ask questions, find opportunities and meet people, with official voices clearly marked and safety built in from day one.

9.1 Principles

  1. Campus first. Your feed is your school, not the whole internet.
  2. Verified by default. Posting needs a verified student account, so the community is made of real students.
  3. Useful over viral. No follower counts as status. Official notices and helpful answers rank above jokes.
  4. Clear sources. Official posts look official. Everything else carries a "not official" signal when it makes claims about the school.
  5. Safe by design. Reporting, blocking, rate limits and moderators exist before the first public post.
  6. Connected to CampusOS. Posts can carry a housing listing, marketplace item, food deal, event or class, so the community feeds the rest of the app.

9.5 The feed and interactions

  • Tabs: For you (ranked), Latest (time order), Official, and later Following.
  • Ranking v1: time order, with pinned official notices first. Ranking v2: a score from freshness, replies and helpful marks, same department, and author trust. Strong anti-clickbait rules: no boost for hate or fighting posts, and reported posts rank down fast.
  • Interactions: like, comment (one level of replies), repost (with optional comment), bookmark, share link, report. On questions, a "helpful" mark replaces likes on answers.
  • Mentions and notifications: replies, mentions and official notices notify. A daily "Campus today" digest at about 7 PM summarised by AI, with a switch to turn it off.
  • Sharing outside the app: each post has a web preview page, so a link pasted into WhatsApp shows the post text and image and opens in the app if installed. This is the main growth loop in Nigeria.
  • Where it lives in the app: the tab bar is full, so the first release adds a "Campus" section at the top of Home, and a dedicated tab is added once the pilot shows it is used daily.

9.6 Trust and safety

Rules and enforcement

  • Plain-language community guidelines shown at first post and linked from every post menu: no harassment, hate, threats, explicit content, scams, impersonation, exam leaks or fake official news.
  • Report with a reason (harassment, scam, false information, explicit, spam, self-harm, other) and an optional note.
  • Auto-protection: a post reported by 3 different verified users is hidden pending review. Posts from new accounts have a lower threshold. A reporter who repeatedly files false reports loses reporting weight.
  • Review queue in the portal: school moderators see their own school's reports, CampusOS staff see everything. Actions: remove, warn, mute for 24 hours or 7 days, suspend posting, ban. Every action is logged with a reason, and the person can appeal once.
  • Strikes: 3 strikes in 90 days means a posting suspension. Severe harm skips straight to a ban.

Prevention

  • Rate limits: new accounts can post 3 times per day and comment 20 times per hour. Normal accounts 10 posts per day and 60 comments per hour.
  • Automatic checks: word and pattern filters (slurs, account numbers, phone numbers in public posts, "send money" patterns), duplicate and spam detection, link checks.
  • Image checks (phase 2): a vision model flags explicit images on upload before they go public.
  • AI-assisted triage: a cheap model sorts the report queue by severity so humans see the worst first. Humans make every final decision.
  • Block and mute: students can block anyone, hide a user's posts, and limit who can message them (message requests for people they have not chatted with).

Fake news and rumours

  • Only official accounts can post a Notice. Anyone else who posts school-related claims sees a gentle "Not official. Check the Official channel" label with a link.
  • Corrections: a post can be marked "Corrected" by a moderator with a link to the official source.

Young users

  • JAMB and WAEC candidates may be under 18. They get their own exam-prep space, no university feed, stricter message requests, and no location sharing.

Legal and policy

  • Update the terms and privacy policy for user content, a takedown process (valid reports acted on within 24 hours), and a route for law-enforcement requests.
  • Respect the Nigerian data protection rules: collect only what is needed, let people delete their posts and account, and keep moderation logs for a limited period.

9.7 Screens (student app)

  • Campus home (feed with tabs), composer (type picker, media, preview), post detail with comments, profile page for a person (posts, department, badge), space pages, bookmarks, notifications for replies, mentions and official notices, report sheet, block and mute settings, and community settings (digest, notifications).

9.8 Portal and admin screens

  • School authority: create official notices, see their school's reports, remove posts, mute or suspend students (with a reason), manage union accounts.
  • CampusOS admin: global report queue, appeals, ban list, keyword list editor, per-school on/off switch, daily stats, audit log.

  • Counters (likes, comments) are kept by database triggers so the feed loads with one query. Paging uses a cursor on time, not page numbers.
  • Storage: a private media folder with signed links, images compressed and size-limited.
  • Realtime: only a "new posts" pill and live comment counts, not the whole feed, to keep cost down.
  • Feature flag: a per-school switch so we can turn the community on for one campus and off instantly if needed.

9.10 Connecting to what we already have

  • School announcements and class announcements appear in the Official channel automatically.
  • "Share to campus" buttons on housing, marketplace, food deals, classes and events.
  • An event post can be added to a student's timetable and the AI study plan will see it.
  • The tutor can answer a question post on request ("Ask the tutor"), with the answer clearly marked as AI.
  • Verification, reports and admin pages already exist and are extended, not rebuilt.

9.12 Pilot and launch plan

  1. Choose one campus where CampusOS has the most active students and a friendly school contact.
  2. Recruit moderators: 5 to 10 trusted students plus one staff contact from the school authority, trained on the guidelines.
  3. Seed the feed before opening: school announcements, a welcome post from the team, a few useful questions and events.
  4. Open for 2 weeks to verified students at that campus only. Review reports daily.
  5. Measure, fix, then open to the next campus.

9.13 Costs to watch

  • Image storage and downloads (controlled by compression and size limits).
  • Database growth for posts and reactions (cursor paging, archive old posts after a long period).
  • AI costs for digests and report triage (small models, caching, daily limits).
  • Moderator time. This is the real cost of any community, so it is planned from the start.
