1. Summary

This plan turns your latest ideas into one build order. The core idea: CampusOS becomes the place where a student's school life happens. Lecturers run department classes, student leaders speak officially, tutors teach in Rooms, students meet each other in the Community, and everything is connected to the wallet, chat and notifications we already have.

3. Who is who: one roles model

Every new feature depends on knowing who someone is at their school. We define this once, then rooms, community, badges and notifications all read from it.

RoleHow they get itWhat they can doProfile badge
Prep studentSigns up as exam candidateJAMB, WAEC and Post-UTME study, prep rooms, prep community, walletNone
In-school studentSigns up with a school or taps "Got admitted"Everything for their school: classes, school community, rooms for their schoolVerified tick when verified
Class repAppointed by the class lecturer (student accepts)Class announcements, take attendance, share materials, start a class session, pinned posts in the classClass rep · CSC 300L
Student leaderAppointed by the school authority (student accepts)Official posts and announcements for their scope (school, faculty, department or hall), free official rooms, pollsSUG President, VP, PRO and others
LecturerApplies, approved by the school or CampusOS (exists)Department classes, students, class reps, attendance, materials, quizzes, live classesLecturer · Department
Tutor NewApplies in the portal, approved by CampusOSCreates rooms (free or paid), live classes, quizzes, debates, earns moneyTutor (Verified tutor after checks)
School authoritySigns up as a school, approved by CampusOS (exists)Student list, verify students, appoint student leaders, official notices, moderate the school communityOfficial · School name
BusinessApplies in the portal (exists)Sells food, housing or goods. Does not post in the community feedBusiness name
CampusOS teamInternalApproves applications, moderates everywhere, handles appealsCampusOS

3.1 Rules that apply everywhere

  • A person can hold several roles. For example a 400L student can be a class rep and an approved tutor. Their profile shows the most important badge first, and the rest under "Roles".
  • Every appointed role has a scope (which class, department, faculty or school) and a term (start and end date). Badges disappear when the term ends or the role is removed.
  • Appointed roles need the person to accept, so nobody is given a public title without knowing.
  • Badges are calculated on the server from the roles tables. The app never decides who has a badge.
  • Every appointment and removal is written to the audit log with who did it.

3.2 Where badges appear

Profile header, people search, feed posts, comments, room member lists, room messages, class announcements, and the official channel. Tapping a badge explains it, for example "Class rep for CSC 300L, appointed by Dr Bello, 2026/2027 session".

4. Joining a school: Got admitted and new sign-ups

4.1 Got admitted (prep student becomes in-school)

Tap "Got admitted?"› Choose school (always asked again)› Campus› Faculty and department› Level (default 100L) and session› Matric number (optional now)› Confirm› Welcome to your school
  • The school picker opens empty, not on the school chosen during exam prep, because students are often admitted somewhere else. The prep target school is shown as a shortcut ("Use University of Ibadan"), not a default.
  • If the school is not on CampusOS yet, the student can request it (the institution request flow exists) and continue with the school name. They are connected automatically when the school is added.
  • One server function does the whole switch in one step, so a dropped network cannot leave the account half-switched: it changes the account to in-school, saves the school, campus, department and level, sets the Premium price to the school rate, then runs the assignment in 4.3.
  • Prep study history (CBT results, notes, flashcards) is kept. Prep rooms remain until their paid period ends, then leave the Rooms list.
  • The app reloads into the in-school side: courses, timetable, CGPA, classes and the school community.

4.2 New in-school sign-up

The current onboarding asks for school and campus. It will also ask for faculty, department and level, then run the same assignment as 4.3. Students who signed up before this change are asked once on Home: "Add your department to join your classes".

4.3 Automatic assignment

StepWhat happens
SchoolThe student is added to the school's student list with status "Pending verification" (or "Verified" if their school email domain matches, as today).
School authority notifiedIf the school authority is on CampusOS, they get an in-app notification and a daily digest: "12 new students joined today (5 in Computer Science)". Not one push per student, to avoid noise.
Department classesThe student is enrolled in every active class for their department, level and current session where the lecturer turned on auto-join (the auto-join switch already exists on classes). They see the classes immediately under Chat › Rooms and in their timetable.
LecturersEach lecturer sees the new student in their class list, with a weekly summary rather than a push per student.
CommunityThe student joins the school feed, the department space and the official channel.
StudentOne welcome notification: "You're in. 4 classes from Computer Science were added."

Edge cases

  • Department changes (for example a change of course): the student leaves auto-joined classes of the old department and joins the new ones. Classes they joined by code stay.
  • Level changes each new session: a scheduled job prompts "Are you now in 200L?" and moves auto-joined classes after the student confirms.
  • Wrong school: the student can change school once without support. Later changes go through support, to stop people hopping between schools.
  • Lecturer creates a class after students joined: when auto-join is on, existing matching students are added at once.

Effort: 3 to 4 days (part of phase 1).

5. Lecturer classes and class reps

Lecturers already have classes, enrolments, announcements, materials, attendance, grades and live sessions in the portal. This phase makes them department-based, adds class reps, and brings the important actions to the phone.

5.1 Creating a class

  • Lecturer dashboard › New class: name (for example "CSC 301, Computer Science 300L"), course code, department (one or more), level, session and semester, schedule, venue, and the switches below.
  • Auto-join (on by default): students of that department and level join automatically (section 4.3).
  • Add students manually: search the school's student list, paste matric numbers, upload a CSV, or share the join code and QR code.
  • Join approval (optional): students who join by code wait for the lecturer or rep to approve.
  • Each class gets a class room in the student's Chat › Rooms tab, with the same tools as tutor rooms (messages, polls, files, live classes, quizzes), marked "Official class" and always free.

5.2 Appointing a class rep

Lecturer opens class › Students› Make class rep› Student gets an invitation› Student accepts› Badge appears on profile
  • Up to two reps per class (rep and assistant rep). A school authority can also appoint reps for any class in their school.
  • Rep powers in that class only: post announcements (shown with the Class rep badge and pushed to the class), pin messages, share materials, take attendance, start a class session or voice call, create polls, and approve join requests if the lecturer allows it.
  • Rep limits: cannot see grades, private student data or other classes. Cannot remove the lecturer or other reps.
  • Announcements by a rep show "Class rep · CSC 300L" next to the name in the class, in the feed's Official tab (for that class's students) and on the rep's profile.
  • The lecturer can remove the rep at any time. The role ends automatically when the session ends.

5.3 Attendance

MethodHow it works
Code (new default)The lecturer or rep opens attendance. The app shows a 4-digit code that changes every 30 seconds and a QR code. Students enter the code or scan it within the time window (for example 10 minutes). This stops students marking from their hostel when friends send the code, because the code expires quickly.
Location check (optional)The lecturer can require students to be within, say, 150 metres of the venue. Off by default because GPS indoors is unreliable.
ManualTick students from the list (exists today), useful for small classes or when phones are not allowed.
OfflineIn the Business app, the lecturer can mark attendance with no network. It syncs when the phone is back online.
  • Reports: attendance per student and per session, percentage per student, "below 75%" list, CSV export from the portal.
  • Students see their own attendance per class and get a gentle warning at 75%.

Effort: 4 to 5 days (part of phase 1).

6. School authority and student leaders

6.1 The school's student list

A new "Students" page in the school authority dashboard (portal and Business app) lists every student who signed up with that school name.

  • Search by name, matric number or email. Filter by faculty, department, level, verified or not, and roles held.
  • Each row: photo, name, department, level, verification status, joined date, roles.
  • Actions: verify or reject (exists in the verifications page), appoint to an office, appoint as class rep, view attendance summary, restrict from the community (with a reason).
  • Counts at the top: total students, new this week, verified share, by faculty.
  • Privacy: the authority sees school-related details only, not wallet, orders, housing, chats or AI history.

6.2 Student offices

ScopeDefault offices (the school can rename or add its own)
School (SUG)President, Vice President, General Secretary, Assistant General Secretary, Financial Secretary, Treasurer, Public Relations Officer, Welfare Director, Sports Director, Social Director
FacultyFaculty President, Faculty Vice President, Faculty General Secretary
DepartmentDepartment President, Department Vice President, Class governor (if the school uses one)
Hall or hostelHall Chairman, Hall Secretary
OtherCustom title, for example "Senate Representative"
  • Appointing: choose the office, scope and term dates, pick the student from the list, and send. The student accepts in the app. The badge appears immediately.
  • Handover: when a new person is appointed to a single-holder office (such as President), the old holder becomes "Former President" when the new one accepts.
  • The school can show its leaders on a public "Student leaders" page in the app (for example School › Leaders).

6.3 What student leaders can do in the student app

  • Official announcements to their scope: everyone at the school (SUG offices), a faculty, a department or a hall. Announcements appear in the Feeds › Official tab, pinned for 48 hours, with a push notification that respects notification settings.
  • Polls and surveys for their scope, with one vote per verified student.
  • Official rooms for free, for example "SUG Town Hall", with live voice sessions.
  • Events with RSVP and "add to my timetable".
  • Limits: up to 3 pushed announcements per day per office, to prevent spam. The school authority can see and remove any announcement and suspend an office holder's posting rights.

6.4 School authority actions in the app

Post official notices (exists, extended to the new feed), appoint and remove offices and reps, see reports from their school's community, remove posts, and restrict students from posting (with a reason and an end date). Every action is logged.

Effort: 3 to 4 days (part of phase 1).

8. Rooms

In one line. A Room is a tutor's (or lecturer's, or student leader's) learning space: a group chat plus scheduled live voice or video classes, materials, polls, quizzes and debates, with clear rules about who can join, what it costs and who can speak.

8.1 Kinds of rooms

KindCreated byPriceWho can see it
Tutor roomApproved tutorFree, one-time or monthlyChosen audience (8.3)
Official classLecturer (section 5)Always freeStudents of that class
Official roomStudent leader or school authorityAlways freeTheir scope (school, faculty, department or hall)

8.2 Creating a room (tutor)

StepFields
1. BasicsTitle, subject or course code, short description (what students will learn), cover image, category (University course, JAMB, WAEC, Post-UTME, Skills such as coding or design, Languages, Other), language
2. AudienceIn-school students (choose one school, several schools, or any school), prep students, or both; optional department and level filters. Section 8.3.
3. PriceFree, one-time price, or monthly price. "Students must pay before joining" (default) or "Free preview" (join free for 3 days or 2 classes, then pay to stay). Optional discount for the first month.
4. ScheduleRepeating class times (for example Tue and Thu, 7 to 8 PM), start and end dates, and for each class: voice or video. Extra one-off classes can be added any time.
5. RulesJoin mode (open, approval needed, invite only), capacity (waitlist when full), who can send messages, who can share files, slow mode, room rules text
6. TeamCo-hosts (other tutors) and moderators (trusted members)
7. Preview and publishSee exactly what students will see in Discover. Save as draft or publish.

8.3 Audience rules

  • In-school: only in-school students see the room in Discover. If the tutor picks specific schools, only students of those schools see it.
  • Prep: only prep students (JAMB, WAEC, Post-UTME candidates) see it.
  • Both: allowed for skills rooms (for example coding), not for course rooms.
  • The rule is enforced on the server: a student outside the audience cannot open, join or search the room, even with a link. They see "This room is for another group of students".
  • When a prep student switches to in-school, rooms they already paid for stay until the period ends.

8.4 The room page in Discover (before joining)

  • Cover, title, category, audience label (for example "In-school · UNILAG · 200L Computer Science").
  • Price and what it covers ("₦1,500 a month, renews from wallet, cancel any time"), or "Free".
  • Who it's for, what you'll learn (tutor's list), and the schedule with voice or video per class, shown in the student's local time, with clashes against their timetable highlighted.
  • The admin: tutor profile card (photo, badge, subjects, rating, students taught, years teaching), co-hosts.
  • Inside: number of members, classes held, materials count, a sample of pinned materials and the last few announcements (not the chat).
  • Reviews: rating and recent reviews from members.
  • Rules, refund policy and a Report button.
  • Join button: "Join free", "Join for ₦1,500", "Request to join", or "Join waitlist". Paid joins open the payment confirmation with payment password or fingerprint, as for other wallet payments.

TabWhat it contains

ClassesUpcoming and past classes. "Join live" button that opens the voice or video class (built on the live session system we already have). Reminders 15 minutes before. Attendance recorded automatically when someone joins a live class.
QuizzesOwner only creates. Timed, auto-marked, results and leaderboard (section 8.7).
DebatesOwner only creates (section 8.8).

8.7 Quizzes (owner only)

  • Question types: multiple choice, true or false, short answer (marked by exact match or by the owner), and image questions.
  • Settings: time limit, open and close time, attempts allowed, shuffle questions and options, show results immediately or after closing, leaderboard on or off.
  • AI draft: the owner pastes a note or picks a file from the room, the AI drafts questions, and the owner edits before publishing.
  • Fair play: answer key and timer on the server, screenshots blocked during the quiz on Android, one active attempt.
  • Results: score and corrections for the student; averages, hardest questions and "who hasn't taken it" for the owner.

8.8 Debates (owner only)

  • Set up: motion (for example "AI should be allowed in exams"), two sides, speakers per side (picked by the owner or by "raise hand"), speaking time per turn, number of rounds, and voice or video.
  • Live: the app shows whose turn it is, a countdown, and automatically unmutes the current speaker. The owner can pass or extend a turn. The audience reacts and asks questions in a side chat.
  • End: audience vote, winner announced, optional AI summary of the main arguments posted in the room chat.
  • Text debate option: rounds as posts with word limits, for rooms that prefer not to go live.

8.9 Room lifecycle and membership states

Room: Draft › Published › Paused (no new joins, existing members keep access) › Archived (read-only, files kept for 90 days).
Member: Requested › Active › Expired (monthly payment not renewed: chat and classes locked, files hidden, can renew any time) › Left, Removed or Banned.

8.10 Fairness and protection

  • Tutor earnings from a room are held as Pending and become Available after the protection window (section 13).
  • If a tutor misses 2 scheduled classes in a month without rescheduling, members can claim a refund for that month and the room is flagged for review.
  • Reviews are open only to members who attended at least one class.
  • Reports on rooms, messages and tutors go to the CampusOS moderation queue.

Effort: rooms core 11 to 14 days (phase 3), quizzes, debates, groups and reviews 8 to 10 days (phase 4).

9. Community: Discover, Feeds and People

9.1 Discover

  • Sections: Live now, For your courses (matches the student's courses and department), Popular at your school, Free rooms, Exam prep (prep students only), New tutors, Official rooms, and categories.
  • Search and filters: subject or course code, tutor name, price (free, under ₦1,000, under ₦3,000), voice or video, schedule (mornings, evenings, weekends), rating.
  • Every card shows price, audience, tutor, rating and next class time. Tapping opens the room page in 8.4.
  • Students only see rooms whose audience includes them (8.3).

9.2 Feeds (works like X)

PartHow it works

PostsUp to 500 characters, up to 4 photos or 1 short video (up to 60 seconds), polls, and link cards. Premium does not change post length, to keep the feed equal.
InteractionsReply (threads, as on X), Repost, Quote post, Like, Bookmark, Share (link with preview for WhatsApp), and a view count.
ThreadsThe author can chain posts into a thread. Replies show as conversations under the post.
Mentions and hashtags@mentions notify. Hashtags are searchable, and "Trending at your school" shows the top hashtags and topics of the day.
Who can replyPer post: Everyone at my school, People I follow, or People I mention, like on X.
Official postsBadge, pinned at the top of Official for 48 hours, and cannot be quote-posted to mock (they can be reposted).

Ranking (For you)Version 1: recent posts from your school and people you follow, official first, with likes and replies as a small boost. Version 2: also department, interests and reading time. Reported posts drop down fast. No boost for fights.
ScopeIn-school students see their school's feed. Prep students see a separate prep feed (section 9.4). A national feed for big news can come later.

9.3 People

  • Find people at your chosen school, filtered by faculty, department, level, interests, hostel area and rooms in common. "Suggested for you" shows classmates first.
  • Follow (one-way, for the feed, like X) and Connect (two-way, both accept). Connected people can message each other directly. Others send a message request that lands in Chat › Message requests.
  • Profile: photo, name, @handle, badges, department and level (each can be hidden), bio, interests, posts, rooms they teach (tutors), mutual connections.
  • Privacy settings: appear in People (on or off), who can message me (everyone at my school, connections only, nobody), show my department and level, show my rooms.
  • Safety: block, mute, report, and limits on connect requests (20 a day for new accounts) to stop spam.

9.4 Protecting younger users (prep students)

JAMB and WAEC candidates can be under 18. Rules:

  • Prep students only see and find other prep students in People and Feeds. In-school students and tutors cannot find them in People.
  • Tutors cannot send private messages to prep students. They talk in the room, where moderators can see. A prep student can message a tutor about a room, and the tutor can reply in that thread only.
  • Prep students' profiles show first name and initial only, no school or location by default.
  • Stricter automatic filters on prep content, and reports from prep students reviewed first.

Why this order

  • Roles first: rooms, community badges, official announcements and notifications all depend on knowing who is a rep, leader, lecturer or tutor.
  • Chat before rooms: a room is a chat with extra tabs, so building attachments, polls and permissions once makes rooms faster and keeps one messaging system.
  • Rooms before Feeds: rooms earn money and are easier to moderate (owners moderate their own space). Feeds need the moderation tools and a pilot.
  • Business app in parallel: it can start after phase 3, since tutors and lecturers need it most once rooms are live. Restaurants (step A) can move earlier if orders grow fast.

Release routine (every phase)

  1. Database migrations applied and checked with rolled-back tests for money and permissions.
  2. Server functions deployed before the app that needs them.
  3. Type-check, then a real-phone test on a low-end Android (Tecno or Infinix).
  4. New APK, website download link updated, "What's new" note in the app.
